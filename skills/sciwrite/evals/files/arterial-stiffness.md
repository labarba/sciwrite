# Obesity blunts the reduction in arterial stiffness after aerobic training

## Abstract

Arterial stiffness predicts cardiovascular events, and aerobic exercise reduces
it in most adults. We tested whether obesity blunts this effect. We enrolled 96
adults in a 12-week supervised aerobic training program and measured
carotid–femoral PWV before and after training. PWV decreased by 0.9 m/s in the
normal-weight group and by 0.4 m/s in the obese group (difference between
groups, p = 0.01). Obesity may limit the vascular benefit of aerobic training.

## Methods

We enrolled 92 adults aged 40–65 years and classified them by body mass index
(BMI) into a normal-weight group (BMI 18.5–24.9 kg/m²; n = 48) and an obese
group (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²; n = 44). Obesity affects between 15% and 62% of adults in
high-income countries [12]. Participants attended three supervised 45-minute
cycling sessions per week for 12 weeks. Blood samples were collected after an
overnight fast. We measured carotid–femoral pulse wave velocity (PWV) by
applanation tonometry at baseline and at 12 weeks.

## Results

Thirty-nine of the 44 participants in the obese group (89%) and 45 of the 48 in
the normal-weight group (92%) completed the program. PWV decreased by 0.9 m/s in
the normal-weight group (Table 2). In heavier participants, the decrease was
0.4 m/s.

completed the program.

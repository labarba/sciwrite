## 3. Results

Figure 3 shows the mixing index along the channel for the three geometries. The
mixing index at the outlet was 0.92 for the herringbone channel, 0.61 for the
serpentine channel and 0.34 for the straight channel (Table 2). This is because
the herringbone grooves generate transverse vortices that stretch and fold the
interface between the two fluids. The pressure drop was highest in the
serpentine channel (18.4 kPa), followed by the herringbone (12.1 kPa) and
straight (9.7 kPa) channels.

## 4. Discussion

We fabricated three microchannel geometries by soft lithography and measured
mixing with confocal microscopy at Reynolds numbers from 1 to 50. The mixing
index at the outlet was 0.92 for the herringbone channel, 0.61 for the
serpentine channel and 0.34 for the straight channel, and the pressure drop was
12.1, 18.4 and 9.7 kPa, respectively. Herringbone grooves have been used for
mixing since the work of Stroock et al. [8], who reported mixing lengths similar
to ours. Our results prove that herringbone channels are the optimal design for
all lab-on-a-chip applications.

## 1. Introduction

Wind farms are often built in rows to make the most of limited land. There are
several studies that have shown that the wake of an upstream turbine reduces
the power output of downstream turbines by up to 40% [3, 4]. However, wakes also
increase the fatigue loads on downstream blades because of the added turbulence
[5]. Offshore sites have stronger and steadier winds than onshore sites, and
installation costs at these sites have fallen by half in the last decade [6].
The wake effect was not considered important in early wind farm layouts, which
placed turbines as close together as the land allowed.

Using large-eddy simulation, the wake of a single turbine was resolved in our
previous work [7], and the predicted velocity deficit agreed with wind tunnel
measurements to within 5%, but that study considered only uniform inflow,
whereas real atmospheric boundary layers are sheared and turbulent, and the wake
recovers faster in turbulent inflow because of enhanced mixing, which matters
for turbine spacing. This motivates the present study. Here we simulate a row of
three turbines in a sheared, turbulent inflow and measure how quickly each wake
recovers.
